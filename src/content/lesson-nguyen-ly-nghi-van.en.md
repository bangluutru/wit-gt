## PART 1: GENERAL INFORMATION

### A. Topic Objectives & Target Audience

- **Value of the topic:** Helps learners understand the operating mechanism of inner transformation through the pathway of Question - Inquiry - Realisation - Understanding - Transformation. It provides the cognitive foundation to clearly distinguish between positive and negative inquiry based on one's own desires as the standard frame of reference.
- **Significance for learners:** Completely eliminates the unconscious habit of asking questions and thinking in opposition to what one desires. It shifts learners from a state of confusion and deadlock to the ability to self-direct their lives by mastering their own inquiries.
- **Brought transformation:** Shifting from passively receiving undesirable outcomes to actively creating the future. Learners become a "master of direction" for themselves and their loved ones, knowing how to light up awareness to find answers to their deepest desires.
- **Target Audience:** All individuals aspiring to master their lives, educators, parents, inner consultation experts, and leaders of organizations and enterprises.
- **Format & Duration:** In-person or online, in one intensive session.

### B. In-depth Content: The Principle of Inquiry in Line with Desires – The Secret of Sudden Realisation of Human Life

The previous topics explored the Principle of Light and the Brain Activation Principle; next, we enter the study of the Principle of Inquiry in Line with Desires, also known as the principle of: Question – Inquiry – Realisation – Understanding – Transformation. To begin, there is a word that almost all of us have heard of: "Realisation" (Ngộ) – with levels such as Spiritual Realisation (Giác ngộ), Complete Realisation (Triệt ngộ), and Sudden Realisation (Đốn ngộ). Following the mentor's guidance, there is a sentence we must imprint in our hearts: "Whether a person's life is short or long is not important; what matters is when they realise." Its significance lies in the fact that the moment a person realises something will determine their own change and transformation. Therefore, instead of straining to force superficial changes, the mentor suggests we focus on how to help ourselves "realise" more things.

In any field of life, age or years of experience do not represent excellence. The person who has realised more than others in that field will comprehend it more deeply, and their capacity for action as well as their transformation will be faster than someone who has been doing it for ten or twenty years. We have Spiritual Realisation (deep understanding of the cosmological and human life views), Complete Realisation (thoroughly understanding every aspect of a field), and Sudden Realisation (shortening the time to comprehend something). This learning pathway is designed to prioritize helping people achieve "Sudden Realisation" of human life, shortening 30 to 40 years of life experience into just a few dozen sessions.

But where do wisdom and realisation originate? Humans can realise through three pathways: self-reflection to realise, experiencing (own or others' failures/successes) to realise, and learning to realise. Whichever pathway is taken, the principle indicates: we must realise it ourselves to understand it thoroughly, and from there have a sustainable transformation. Anything in life is best when people are allowed to realise it themselves and act on it, rather than being forced. And fortunately, the mentor has pointed out: A person must have some Inquiry (Nghi vấn) in order to realize.

At this point, we need to distinguish very clearly between a simple "Question" (Hỏi) and an "Inquiry" (Nghi vấn). If you ask, "Did you have lunch?" and the person replies, "Yes, I did," the question is gone after it is asked—that is just a normal question. But an Inquiry is a thought, a question that is repeated over and over in the mind, lingering for days, months, or years, and the brain has a demand to find a solution for it. Once a person has an inquiry, they will realise; if you have no inquiry about a field, you will never achieve realisation in that field. People's lives differ in just one thing: What questions do you ask and what inquiries do you hold?

However, the real danger lies in the duality of inquiry. If you have a Positive Inquiry, the realisation will be positive, the understanding positive, and the life transformation positive. Conversely, if you have a Negative Inquiry, you will realise negatively, understand negatively, and your life will be pushed towards negative transformation. How can we distinguish between Positive and Negative without confusion? Very simply, the mentor points out: Use your own desires as the fixed perspective to inspect. Whatever is in line with our desires is called Positive; whatever goes against our desires is called Negative.

Let's look at this in life: when a child sits too close to the TV, adults usually say, "You are watching too close, watch out or your eyes will be ruined/shortsighted!" That statement comes from care, but if we look at it from our actual desire (wanting bright, healthy eyes), that statement goes against our desire—meaning we are being negative. To speak positively, we must say in line with our desires: "Sit further back so your eyes stay bright." Without using our desires as the reference system, we will never know that we are being negative daily.

Negative words are sometimes not dangerous enough; the real danger is when they become negative inquiries repeated in the head: "Why does my husband treat me so badly?", "Why do I strive so hard but remain poor?". Worrying like this continuously causes all subsequent realisations and understandings to form in darkness. That is why this is called the Principle of Inquiry in Line with Desires: What we want, let us ask questions in that direction and construct inquiries in that direction. Facing an unhappy marriage, instead of tormenting oneself with "Why do I love him but he treats me like this?", change the inquiry to: "How can my husband care for, love his wife and children, and build a harmonious marriage?". One person searches for why there is a problem, the other searches for a solution; in 3 to 5 years, the second person will surely move towards the light.

We do not need to be prophets; just by scanning all the inquiries in our minds, we can fully predict our own future. There are three important things a person needs to manage to master karma and human life: Management of Inner Consciousness (Tâm thức), Management of Thinking (Tư duy), and Management of Behavior (Hành vi). When you thoroughly understand the Principle of Inquiry in Line with Desires, know what you want, and align your inquiries with it, you have officially managed your Thinking. Just by applying this thoroughly, your life is fully directed for the rest of your days.

## PART 2: CORE CONTENT

### 2.1. Concepts

- **Realisation (Ngộ):** The state of finding the solution to one's own concerns and inquiries. Levels of realisation: Small Realisation, Great Realisation, Sudden Realisation (understanding in a short time), Complete Realisation (understanding thoroughly), Spiritual Realisation (understanding a very broad scope).
- **Question (Hỏi):** Simple, normal questions that arise in daily life.
- **Inquiry (Nghi vấn):** A question or thought repeated over many days, months, or years in the mind, where the brain tends to seek a solution for it.
- **Positive and Negative:** Using one's own desires as the fixed perspective for inspection. Whatever is in line with our desires is Positive; whatever goes against our desires is Negative.
- **Positive Inquiry (Inquiry in line with desires):** Thoughts or concerns repeated in the mind in the direction of what we desire.

### 2.2. Key Knowledge

- **The Operating Mechanism of the Principle:** Question → Inquiry → Realisation → Understanding → Transformation. Human transformation originates from deep "understanding", and to understand, one must "realise", which happens because of holding an "inquiry".
- **Three Pathways to Wisdom (Realisation):**
  - Self-reflection to realise (Noble).
  - Experience to realise (Hard, must pay a price).
  - Learning to realise (Fastest, pays the least price).
- **The Duality of Inquiry:**
  - Positive Inquiry leads to Positive Realisation, Positive Understanding, and Positive Transformation.
  - Negative Inquiry leads to Negative Realisation, Negative Understanding, and Negative Transformation.
- **The Power of Inquiry:** The power of a correct positive inquiry is more important and stronger than a thousand answers.
- **Clarifying Desires:** A person needs to clarify their ultimate desires before formulating inquiries. *(Note: Inner conflict will be explored in depth in the topic: Formula for the Root Cause of Life).*

### 2.3. Key Awareness

- **"Whether a person's life is short or long is not important; what matters is when they realise."**
- **No Inquiry, No Realisation:** If you have no inquiry about a field, you will likely have no realisation in that field. A person's realisation depends entirely on the inquiries they hold.
- **The Trap of Unconsciousness:** Most people utter words and set inquiries opposite to what they desire without even knowing it. Identifying positive vs. negative must always adhere to one's own desires as the reference system.
- **Becoming a Master of Direction:** By knowing how to construct inquiries in line with desires, we can not only predict our own future but also act as a coach, helping ourselves and others reconstruct inquiries to light up life.
- **Ultimate Action:** Think in line with desires, Speak in line with desires, Act in line with desires.

### 2.4. Personal Application

- **Example 1 (Health):** Instead of negative concern "Why am I so weak?", change the inquiry to: "How can I have the health of an Ironman?". *Outcome:* The brain automatically searches for physical solutions, and the body changes to become healthier.
- **Example 2 (Inner Being):** Instead of asking "Why do bad things keep happening to me?", change to: "How can I always maintain inner peace, tolerance, and honouring-gratitude?". *Outcome:* The inner being finds peace, achieving "sudden realisation" to rise above adversities.
- **Example 3 (Career):** Instead of complaining "Why doesn't my boss recognize my ability?", change to: "How can I elevate my personal value of creation?". *Outcome:* The brain activates professional development ideas, and capacity and finances automatically grow.

### 2.5. Family Application

- **Example 1 (Husband & Wife):** When the husband often goes out for drinks, instead of asking "Why do you always neglect the family?", the wife changes to: "How can I make him love coming home to have dinner with us?". *Outcome:* The energy frequency shifts, and the husband feels more warmth and connection.
- **Example 2 (Child Behavior Education):** When the child watches the phone too close, instead of scolding "Do you want to be blind?", parents say in line with desires: "Sit further back so your eyes stay bright and healthy!". *Outcome:* The child adjusts voluntarily without fear-based images being planted.
- **Example 3 (Child Maturity):** When the child is lazy to study, instead of worrying "How can I fix his laziness?", parents change to: "How can I guide my child to become an outstanding talent?". *Outcome:* The child finds passion and reveals good potentials.

### 2.6. Organizational Application

- **Example 1 (Personnel Leadership):** Instead of being annoyed "Why are employees working irresponsibly?", leaders set the inquiry: "How can I nurture them to become 9 key types of people in 1?". *Outcome:* Employees maximize their capacity.
- **Example 2 (Company Culture):** Instead of struggling "How to eliminate negative staff?", the board inquiries: "How can we build a happy, hopeful working environment?". *Outcome:* The environment transforms, and staff align in language and consensus.
- **Example 3 (Sustainable Organization):** Instead of asking "How to avoid bankruptcy?", change to: "How can we create a sustainable organization that brings value to society?". *Outcome:* The organization finds a new guide star and attracts a group of distinguished minds.

### 2.7. Social Impact

- **Example 1 (Educational Value):** Instead of complaining "Young people nowadays are living so astray", change to: "How can we help 25 million Vietnamese children comprehend cause, condition, and outcome?". *Outcome:* Creates a strong wave of cognitive transformation in the community.
- **Example 2 (Community Responsibility):** Instead of asking "Why are they still poor despite constant charity?", change to: "How can we help them stand above emerging adversities?". *Outcome:* Shifts from "material charity" to "cognitive charity", helping the community rise sustainably.
- **Example 3 (International Connection):** Instead of deadlocking "How to stop conflicts?", direct the inquiry to: "How can we create a world of shared language and consensus?". *Outcome:* Erases boundaries, connecting people through love and truth.
