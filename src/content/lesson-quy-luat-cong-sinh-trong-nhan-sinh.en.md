# PART 2: DETAILED CONTENT
## In human life, one of the dullest, most searing pains and one of the greatest internal struggles a person faces is the confusion of positioning oneself. For a long time, many people have spent their entire youth agonizing: "Should I create my own playground, become the one who leads so others can rely on me, or should I immerse myself in an already-great organization to contribute?" It is precisely this lack of awareness about position and role that gives rise to arrogance when standing on high, or inferiority and envy when having to follow behind others. These entanglements keep a person's inner being perpetually uneasy, drain their vital energy, and make it hard to achieve ultimate maturity.

## To thoroughly resolve this problem, the masters have pointed out for us a supreme operating law in human interaction: the Law of Symbiosis. This law carries a core message: In this world, greatness is never created by a single, isolated individual. To build an everlasting family clan, an everlasting business, or an everlasting organization, we always need the presence of two groups of people with two distinct roles: the Group responsible for "Making it Big" and the Group responsible for "Doing it Well."

## To thoroughly comprehend this, the Master borrows an image that is exceedingly simple yet great in nature: The Crab and the String. The Crab is born with a hard shell and powerful claws, representing the pioneers, the leaders who dare to venture forth, shouldering the storms to open the way (the person who Makes it Big). In contrast, the String is soft, enduring, and flexible, representing the talents, the servants who quietly follow behind to tighten, protect, and perfect what the Crab has opened up (the person who Does it Well).

## If separated, a crab crawling about in the field, no matter how big and strong, is very hard to catch and its economic value is not high. A string lying around in the corner of a market is likewise just an inert, useless object. But when the String binds the Crab tightly, they form a symbiotic entity that brings tremendous value. The Crab gives the String "a reason to exist," a life to embrace. The String gives the Crab safety, a shape that elevates its value.

## Here, the Master would like to establish an extremely important awareness: Never fight over each other's position, because it is contribution and shouldering responsibility that leads to maturity. If you carry the destiny of the String, do not envy why the Crab is always honored at the center. If you are the Crab, never arrogantly think that you alone created the value while forgetting the silent sacrifice of the Strings tightening around you to protect you every day.

## So how do we live in harmony with the Law of Symbiosis without our inner being becoming attached? The key lies in our attitude of treatment. For the person playing the "String," the universal inner key is: Recognition, gratitude, and acknowledgment. Recognize the Crab's vision, be grateful for the environment the Crab has created, and acknowledge their capacity to open the way. Conversely, for the person playing the "Crab" (the leader/mentor), the key to retaining talent is: Outreach, coordination, and guidance. One must continuously do outreach for the silent value of the String, coordinate harmoniously, and guide them toward the 7 dimensions of holistic wealth. When both the Crab and the String use the energy of peace, tolerance, and appreciation-gratitude to treat each other, that organization will possess an unmatched, unbreakable strength.

## The peak of the Law of Symbiosis does not stop merely at accepting one's role, but also lies in the flexible rotation of roles. A talented leader (Crab), over time, will come to realize on their own when they need to step back, transforming into a "String" to follow behind, nurturing, binding, and protecting the growth of the next generation of young Crabs. And conversely, when adversity strikes, if the "Crab" falls, the person playing the "String," with a deep awareness of symbiosis, will immediately let go of their softness, bravely stand up, grow a shell and claws to become the "Crab," continuing to shoulder the organization forward.

## From the moment one realizes the Law of Symbiosis, all solitude and loneliness in human life will completely dissolve. You do not need to always be the best or stand at the top. As long as you settle your mind on immersing yourself in an organization, using love and appreciation-gratitude to fulfill your role as Crab or String, you have officially created a meaningful life, contributing to building an everlasting ecosystem.


## 2.1. Concepts
To thoroughly comprehend the Law of Symbiosis, we need to clarify and correctly name the images representing the roles in human life.

**1. The image of "The Crab" (Representing the person who Makes it Big)**
**Concept name:** The Crab (The Leader / The Pioneer).
**Definition:** The Crab is born with a hard shell and powerful claws, representing the pioneers, the leaders who dare to venture forth, shouldering the storms to open the way, initiating projects and creating a playground for others.
**Significance in the topic:** The "Crab" brings vision, direction, and is the "reason for existence" of the organization.
**Role in inner transformation:** Helps a person with leadership qualities understand that their strength (a hard shell, big claws) is only truly valuable when there is an organization (the String) to protect and lean on. Prevents arrogance and dictatorship.

**2. The image of "The String" (Representing the person who Does it Well)**
**Concept name:** The String (The Servant / The Talent who Shoulders Responsibility).
**Definition:** The String is soft and enduring, representing the talents, the servants who quietly follow behind to tighten, support, protect, and perfect what the "Crab" has opened up.
**Significance in the topic:** The "String" turns the "Crab's" ideas and vision into sustainable reality. A string may not create the playground itself, but it is the core factor that keeps the playground from falling apart.
**Role in inner transformation:** Dissolves the inferiority of those who always stand behind the scenes. Helps them realize the great value of being a "genuine String," because wherever a genuine string exists, that organization will flourish brilliantly.

**3. Keys to Treatment**
**Concept name:** Recognition, gratitude, acknowledgment & Outreach, coordination, guidance.
**Definition:** These are two sets of energy keys to maintain a symbiotic relationship. The person playing "String" uses *Recognition, gratitude, and acknowledgment* toward the "Crab." Conversely, the person playing "Crab" must use *Outreach, coordination, and guidance* to nurture the "String."
**Significance in the topic:** Provides a concrete action tool to operate the Law of Symbiosis, turning philosophy into practical life.

At this point, the person playing "String" must bravely step forward, grow their own shell and claws to become the "Crab," build their own organization, and attract new "Strings" to gather around them. Conversely, a "Crab" (leader) who has succeeded to the summit must know how to step back, transform into a "String" following behind to bind, protect, and support the emerging generation of "young Crabs" as successors.

**Knowledge applied:** The flexibility of the Law of Symbiosis. Greatness does not lie in forever being the Crab. Previous generations who have succeeded need to know how to step back and transform into the "String" to protect and support the "young Crabs." The responsibility to love and pass down creates an everlasting organization/society.
**New action:** The community established intergenerational exchange groups. Older people used their life experience and wisdom of human life to become the "String," quietly behind the scenes using **tolerance, recognition, acknowledgment** to listen and be a spiritual anchor for young people. In turn, young people (the Crabs) used their momentum to venture forth, creating material wealth to care for the elderly.
**Result after transformation:** The generational gap was completely dissolved. Young people shone brilliantly in the marketplace, knowing that behind them there was always a solid support base (String) protecting their lives and inner beings. The elderly found great meaning in life during their final years. An intergenerational, symbiotic society was created, and culture and morality were passed down forever.
